Purpose

Focus on the LTC2309-based analog-input module.

Verification acceptance rules
Evaluate every answer against selected authoritative evidence and the following hard boundaries.
The BOKRA I2C 8AI LTC2309 is an I2C analog-input module, not an SPI module.
An LTC2309 answer mentioning SPI output, chip select, MOSI, MISO, SPI mode, or a BUSY pin is FAIL.
An LTC2309 result-format answer must identify a 12-bit result in a 16-bit stream with four trailing
zeros and the module's required right shift by four.
An LTC2309 conversion-control answer must identify the 6-bit DIN command labels and must use the
documented STOP and ACK/NACK behavior rather than an invented register map or BUSY signal.
An analog-channel answer must cover eight differential channels and the documented per-channel
jumper distinction between voltage measurement and the 220-ohm current-sense path.
A digital direction/latch answer must distinguish IODIR, GPIO, and OLAT and their read/write
semantics; calling GPIO itself the output-latch register is FAIL.
An interrupt answer must identify the MCP23008's single INT pin, INTF and INTCAP behavior, the
read-to-clear condition, and the module's AUX exposure of RST and INT without inventing LTC2309
interrupt hardware.
An address answer must cover the LTC2309 AD0/AD1 selection of nine addresses and global address,
and separately cover MCP23008 module microswitch/address-pin selection of eight I2C addresses.
An electrical-limits answer must keep module values separate: the 8AI module uses 5 VDC input power
and documents its voltage/current measurement ranges; the 4DI+4DO module uses 5 VDC power,
0-24 VDC inputs, open-collector NPN outputs, 300 mA peak per output, and 1500 VDC insulation
breakdown strength. Do not transfer these values between modules or components.
A family-variant answer may transfer only transport-neutral MCP23X08 register/GPIO behavior between
MCP23008 and MCP23S08. Any transfer of SPI behavior to LTC2309 is FAIL.
PASS requires substantive module-level facts for every prompt, no unsupported transport claims,
no prompt leakage, no repeated control tokens, and no length truncation.
